    How Online Weight Loss Clinics Work, From Questionnaire to Doorstep

    Oakwell MD care team · August 22, 2026 · 6 min read

    An online weight loss clinic replaces the waiting room with a questionnaire and a secure message thread. A licensed provider still decides if you are a candidate, which medication fits and what dose to start. What changes is where it happens. Here is what the process looks like from the first click to the box at your door.

    The process, step by step

    Step one: the health questionnaire

    The intake usually takes 10 to 15 minutes. You will be asked about your weight history, medicines you take now and any drug allergies. Some questions screen for safety, such as any personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease or eating disorders, any of which can change the plan. Pregnancy plans matter too. Semaglutide is usually stopped at least two months before trying to conceive.

    Answer honestly, even about things that feel unrelated. A past episode of binge eating or a recent gallbladder surgery changes what the provider recommends. You will also upload a photo ID. Some clinics ask for a full-length photo or a picture of your scale reading to confirm your current weight before the provider signs off.

    Step two: provider review and labs

    A licensed provider in your state reads your answers. You might be asked to upload ID, a current weigh-in photo, or recent bloodwork like an A1c and kidney function. Labs can often be drawn at a local lab if you do not have recent results. Then the provider decides. Not everyone qualifies, and a good clinic will tell you so instead of finding a workaround.

    Eligibility for FDA-approved weight loss drugs generally means a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition such as high blood pressure or sleep apnea. Insulin and sulfonylureas get extra attention, since pairing them with a GLP-1 can push blood sugar too low. Expect follow-up questions.

    Step three: prescription and pharmacy

    If a medication is appropriate, the provider sends a prescription to a licensed US pharmacy. Brand pens can be filled at a retail pharmacy through insurance, and compounded versions come from a licensed compounding pharmacy. Compounded products are not FDA-approved. Medication that needs refrigeration ships in an insulated box with cold packs. Put it in the fridge the day it arrives.

    Shipping usually takes two to five business days after the pharmacy fills the order. Watch for the tracking email, since cold-packed boxes should not sit on a porch overnight. Brand pens can come with needles included. Compounded vials often ship with syringes and alcohol swabs.

    Step four: ongoing check-ins

    The prescription is the start, not the end. Most plans include a check-in before each dose increase, usually every four weeks during titration. Your provider asks about nausea, bowel habits, your weight trend and how much protein you are getting, then decides whether to step up, hold or lower the dose. Refills depend on those answers.

    Messages between check-ins are part of the service. New symptoms like persistent vomiting or severe abdominal pain should not wait for the scheduled check-in. Weight stalls are common around month three. A plateau usually prompts a review of dose and protein intake, not an automatic increase.

    What a good online clinic does

    • Uses licensed providers who practice in your state.
    • Names the pharmacy filling your prescription and whether the product is FDA-approved.
    • Asks for labs or records when your history calls for them.
    • Makes it easy to message a provider about side effects between visits.
    • Shows pricing clearly after the provider review, without surprise charges.

    Questions patients ask

    Is a video visit required? It depends on your state. Some states require a live video or phone visit before certain prescriptions, while others allow the review to happen through the questionnaire and secure messages. Your clinic should tell you which applies.

    Can you use insurance? Coverage for brand pens depends on your plan, and most insurers want a prior authorization first. Compounded medication is almost always cash-pay. Pricing is shown after the provider review, and it depends on the treatment plan, the medication form and how often you need refills.
